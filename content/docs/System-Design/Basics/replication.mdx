# Replication

Replication keeps multiple copies of data or service state for availability, durability, read locality, or throughput. It does not automatically provide all four, and it is not a backup.

## Design choices

- **Leader-based:** one leader orders writes and followers replicate; failover must prevent two leaders from accepting conflicting writes.
- **Multi-leader:** multiple sites accept writes; conflict detection and resolution become part of the application contract.
- **Leaderless or quorum:** clients or coordinators read and write multiple replicas; quorum settings, repair, and conflict semantics determine behavior.

Synchronous replication can reduce acknowledged data loss while increasing write latency and sensitivity to coordination failure. Asynchronous replication lowers write latency but creates lag and a non-zero recovery point during failover.

## Operational questions

- Which failures can the replica topology survive?
- What does a client observe after writing and reading through another replica?
- How are stale replicas detected, repaired, and prevented from becoming leaders?
- What happens during a network partition or failed failover?
- How are lag, durability, and data divergence measured?

Backups provide a point-in-time copy isolated from live replication and destructive actions. Test restoration and application-level correctness independently of replica failover.
